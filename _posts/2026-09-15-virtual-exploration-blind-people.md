## Abstract

Virtual reality (VR) shows promise in supporting blind people in exploring unfamiliar environments before visiting them in person through multimodal interaction. However, effective exploration requires understanding both geometric route structures and spatial features, raising questions about how blind users perceive these different forms of information and use multiple interaction methods during exploration of complex virtual environments. We present a VR exploration system to convey route structures and spatial features and report findings from two studies with blind participants. In a formative study with six participants, users explored non-linear routes; turns close to 90◦ and curved segments were recognized more frequently, while route reconstruction became more difficult as route complexity increased. In a second study with seven participants, users explored a virtual museum containing landmarks and objects, perceived approximately 60% of key landmarks, and actively combined multiple interaction methods according to their exploration goals and the spatial scale of information they sought. During the subsequent museum visit, participants recognized approximately half of the landmarks conveyed during virtual exploration when prompted with their names (i.e., landmark name). Across both studies, participants developed stronger knowledge of nearby objects, landmarks, and local route relationships than of how these elements related across the broader environment.
